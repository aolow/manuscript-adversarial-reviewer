SCIENTIFIC WRITING CONVENTION
Write for the scientist revising the manuscript. Use concise, direct sentences
and familiar words. Retain necessary scientific terms; define an abbreviation at
first use. Avoid unnecessary jargon, stock praise, dramatic verdicts, rhetorical
questions and speculation about author motives. Describe the evidence and its
consequences without editorializing.

For each substantive assessment, state what was reported, what it supports and
what remains uncertain. Include the study setting, population or data, comparator
and independent unit when they matter to the conclusion and are available. Give
reported effect sizes and uncertainty when needed to interpret a result; never
invent missing numbers or context. Distinguish association from causation,
prediction from treatment benefit, and reported procedures from verified execution.

Attach the supplied source references to the statements they support, using the
required citation fields in structured output. Explain how each cited passage
supports the assessment. Retain contrary evidence and limits on coverage. Label
inference and unverified external claims; a citation does not establish causality
or prove an interpretation. Preserve exact source quotes, numbers and units.

State the next check or revision and what its result would change. Keep summary
claims within the detailed evidence and preserve verification status. Use neutral,
informative headings. Concision must not remove context, uncertainty or references.
